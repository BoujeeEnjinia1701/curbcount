# CurbCount STEM check

Verdict: the working principle (a coarse thermal array that cannot resolve a face, counting warm blobs by class and direction) sits within known science, and so does option B (60 GHz radar), but four details fail and are open. Of the 31 claims checked, **13 are within known science, 14 are plausible and 4 fail with a fix proposed; none fails outright.** The four open failures all touch the shared FieldNode core, so they go to Amish: S5 (a car's warm hood is in the field for 3.2 frames, not the 4 needed; fix: 32 Hz subpages on a 1 MHz bus), S8 (LiFePO4 may not be charged below 0 degrees C, so the node is energy neutral only where the winter air stays above about 0 degrees C), S26 and S28 (option B: a shared cell above its charge limit on hot days, and a charger set for 1.5 A under a 20 W panel). Option B's first drawing also failed (S22 and S23: the radar running continuously drew 32 Wh/day against 14.5 Wh/day stored); the fix, decided under Amish's standing instruction of 2026-10-23, is duty-cycled frame bursts (CBC-DEC-001 C1), tightened to 0.2 W average after the independent review. `project.yaml` records `stem_check: open`. Method and rules: STANDARDS section 20. Calculations: `docs/04-calcs/stem_check.py`, which imports the sizing of CBC-CAL-001.

## Concept options
